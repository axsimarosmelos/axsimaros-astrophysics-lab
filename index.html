About Axsimaros

AN INDEPENDENT LEARNING OBSERVATORY

A little wonder goes a long way.

Axsimaros Astrophysics Lab brings exploration, space news, and learning together in one place. All six interactive scenes are rendered in real 3D with WebGL.

How to explore

Drag a scene to rotate the camera. Scroll, or use the + and − buttons, to zoom. On a focused canvas, arrow keys rotate and + / − zoom. Select a planet to inspect it. Use the time slider, pause button, orbit paths, and spatial grid to investigate motion.

Science and imagery

These are educational models. Planet sizes and distances are adjusted for readability; simulated positions are not live ephemerides. The black-hole shader integrates approximate bent rays with a Doppler brightness model; it is not a validated general-relativity solution. The galaxy uses advected tracers and an analytic emitting volume. The nebula uses an illustrative density field with emission and absorption. Numerical transit photometry uses a linear limb-darkening law. The Saturn hero is an AI-created artist’s visualization. Planet surfaces use image maps; clouds, atmospheres, and volumetric deep-space scenes are rendered procedurally.

Hero art direction: a realistic, softly lit Saturn with detailed icy rings against deep navy space, leaving room for the site’s introduction. It is included with this website.

Image credits

Sources and news

The library links to NASA learning resources. Topic-matched YouTube links open video searches. The newsroom requests current articles from the Spaceflight News API when available, and labels its dated, selected NASA articles when the live service cannot be reached.
